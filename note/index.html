Loops cannot see most registration error: measuring the visibility ceiling of cycle consistency

Russell Parrish, Parallax Metrology. Draft methods note, 20 November 2026. Not peer reviewed. · Code and data · Lab book

Abstract. Consistency and transitivity checks (register A to B, B to C and A to C; see whether the loop closes) are used as label-free quality checks on deformable image registration. True displacements are differences of positions, so the part of a registration set that fails to close is exactly the part of its error that fails to close. A Hodge decomposition of the error, computed wherever truth exists, therefore gives the share of error that any closure-based check can see. If errors were independent per registration, the invisible share on a complete graph of n images would be 2/n. On DIR-Lab thoracic 4DCT (10 patients, all 15 pairs among six phases per landmark) the invisible share is 93.1% for whole-volume Diffeomorphic Demons (750 landmarks; case-clustered 95% interval 90.3 to 96.2%) and 79.1% for a local translation registrar (477 landmarks; 73.3 to 95.8%), against 33.3% expected. Both stay above 70% after a pessimistic allowance for annotation noise, which is itself invisible to loops. On POPI (1 patient, 10 phases) the translation registrar gives 92.9% against 20%, though there annotation noise cannot be excluded as the main contributor. The visible part still names a landmark's worst registration above chance, but no better than an image residual. The invisible share bounds what any closure-only check can report, and it can be measured on a truth-bearing subset before such a check is relied on.

1. Why measure this

Registration quality is hard to check without ground truth, so self-consistency is attractive. Clinical registration QA in radiotherapy is framed by AAPM TG-132 [Brock et al. 2017]. The MIRSIG position paper [Lowther et al. 2022] accepts testing consistency (independence from registration direction) and, where needed, transitivity (independence from the route through three or more images), and notes that passing a consistency tolerance does not by itself establish spatial accuracy. Cycle consistency is also built into recent registration methods [van Harten et al. 2024]. The caveat is widely stated; its size is rarely measured. This note measures it.

2. The identity

Take n images and independent pairwise registrations among them, forming a graph whose nodes are images and whose edges carry measured displacements yij of a material point. The truth is tij = pj − pi, a gradient on the graph. Write the error e = y − t. The Hodge decomposition splits any edge field orthogonally into an integrable (gradient) part and a cyclic part [Jiang et al. 2011]. Because t is purely integrable,

cyclic(y) = cyclic(e).

Every closure statistic built from the measured edges, from one triangle's transitivity residual to cycle residuals on a larger graph, is a function of cyclic(y), so it sees only cyclic(e). We report the invisible share, the fraction of error energy in the integrable part, pooled over landmarks. (This note measures displacements of tracked points; for nonlinear maps the argument applies to the sampled displacements.)

Independent-error baseline. For independent, isotropic edge errors on the complete graph Kn, the integrable subspace has dimension n − 1 per axis out of n(n − 1)/2 edges, so the expected invisible share is 2/n: 66.7% for three images, 33.3% for six, 20% for ten. Simulation reproduces these values (66.7%, 33.2%, 19.9%). A share far above 2/n means errors are organised per image (a node offset, as in Figure 1, middle) rather than per registration.

Annotation noise is invisible too. A landmark placed slightly wrong in one image is a node offset. We bound this with a pessimistic allowance: the published mean repeat-annotation distance d is read as the mean 3-D error of a single annotation, giving expected noise energy 2·3·(d/1.596)2 per edge (1.596 = 2√(2/π)), which is removed from the integrable part.

3. Data and registrars

DIR-Lab 4DCT [Castillo et al. 2009, 2010]: 10 patients, 0.97 to 1.16 mm in-plane, 2.5 mm slices; 75 landmarks per case on the six expiratory phases T00 to T50 (T10 to T40 coordinates are propagated); published per-case repeat-observer errors 0.70 to 1.13 mm. Obtained through DIR-Lab's access form. POPI [Vandemeulebroucke et al. 2007]: one patient, ten phases, 40 expert landmarks, 0.98 mm in-plane, 2 mm slices.

Whole-volume Demons. SimpleITK Diffeomorphic Demons, three-level pyramid (8, 4, 2 mm grids; 80, 60, 40 iterations; HU clipped to [−1000, 400]), fitted independently for all 15 phase pairs of each DIR-Lab case (150 fits; 150 with positive Jacobians throughout the interior). Query points follow the landmark: its T00 position is mapped to each phase with the T00-to-phase field, and each edge is read from its own field at that point. Parameters were fixed in advance, not tuned to these data. Mean edge error 1.99 mm.

Local translation window. Tapered FFT cross-correlation on a 32-voxel window at the T00 landmark, refined by spatial NCC; the same centre in every phase. Kept if the window fit in the volume and passed a fixed texture check: 477 of 750 DIR-Lab landmarks, 28 of 40 POPI landmarks (POPI also used a body-interior guard). Mean edge error on DIR-Lab 2.46 mm.

Every registration was independent, so no loop closes by construction. Only T00 landmarks were read during measurement; measurements were sealed (hashed) before targets were read for scoring, and analysis plans were hashed before each run.

Loop residuals name a landmark's worst registration well above chance. On POPI they did so more often than the image residual; that did not replicate on DIR-Lab, where the two are indistinguishable (difference -6.6 to +2.1 points, case-clustered 95%) and the image residual ranks all edges better (mean within-landmark Spearman 0.30 versus 0.09). Using loops to correct rather than detect fares no better: least-squares synchronisation of all 45 POPI registrations increased phase-10 error by 2.8% and reduced all-pairs error by 2.4%.

6. Where the invisible error comes from (exploratory)

For the window registrar three checks narrow it. Estimator shrinkage is small: exact integer-voxel shifts of real POPI windows are recovered with gains of 0.98, 0.92, 0.93 (z, y, x, refined). Re-centring each window on the estimated landmark position in its source phase left POPI all-pairs error essentially unchanged (1.383 to 1.378 mm). And an unrelated registrar shares the error: on POPI, Demons and the window have error correlations of 0.85, 0.81, 0.90 per axis. With annotation noise bounded on DIR-Lab, the main invisible component is a per-image tracking offset: the registrar follows a trajectory close to, but not the same as, the landmark's, consistently across every pairing (Figure 4).

A pre-registered attempt to read the error shared between the two registrars as annotation noise (triple collocation [Stoffelen 1998]) failed its known-answer test against DIR-Lab's published observer errors: on T00 to T50, estimates ran 1.3 to 5.2 times the published values and tracked motion amplitude. The two registrars share tracking error, which violates that method's independence assumption.

7. Relation to prior work

That consistent errors escape consistency checks is understood qualitatively. Inverse consistency has long been imposed as a desirable property of registration [Christensen and Johnson 2001], yet a consistent registration can still be wrong, as the MIRSIG position paper states for clinical QA [Lowther et al. 2022]. Loop constraints are used to detect erroneous pairwise relations in structure from motion [Zach et al. 2010], which works only where an error breaks closure. Image-similarity surrogates are likewise unreliable proxies for accuracy [Rohlfing 2012]. Synchronisation methods exploit closure on graphs [Singer 2011]. The contribution here is a measurement: the Hodge split of the error on a truth-bearing subset, with an independent-error baseline of 2/n and an annotation-noise allowance, turns the qualitative caveat into a number per registrar and data type.

8. Limits

  • Two registrars, both run with fixed parameters and neither tuned to these data; their DIR-Lab errors (2.5 and 2.0 mm mean edge error over all 15 phase pairs) are above the roughly 1 mm that the best published methods reach on DIR-Lab's extreme phases. Registrars near the annotation floor, including learned ones, may split differently; the measurement applies per registrar.
  • Lung 4DCT only, 11 patients. POPI alone does not separate registration error from annotation noise, and its targets had been examined in a preceding pilot.
  • Energy pooling weights large errors heavily; per-landmark medians are reported beside pooled shares.
  • DIR-Lab intermediate-phase landmarks are propagated rather than independently annotated; their error is per-image and therefore inside the invisible share and the allowance.
  • Demons query points come from the registrar's own T00-to-phase estimates, as they would in use.
  • Everything except the pre-registered covariance test in section 6 (which failed) is exploratory.

9. Reproducibility

Code and per-landmark results: github.com/parallaxmetrology/loop-visibility. python reproduce.py recomputes every number in this note from the per-landmark files (81 checks; no images or landmark files needed). The repository also holds the analysis plans with the SHA-256 hashes recorded before each run, the exact study scripts, and standalone copies of both registrars and of the Hodge split, verified to reproduce the study's outputs exactly on DIR-Lab data. The Demons run was interrupted by an out-of-memory crash after five cases; cases 6 to 10 were completed by a memory-lean script with identical parameters and query rule, declared and hashed before it ran. DIR-Lab and POPI images, landmark coordinates and raw measurements are not redistributed; DIR-Lab data are available from its authors on request. Analysis code was written with AI coding assistants (OpenAI Codex for the preceding pilot, Anthropic Claude for this study).
